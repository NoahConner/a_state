Texas Auto Insurance Requirements — What Houston Drivers Must Carry

State Minimum Coverage: 30/60/25 Explained

Texas requires drivers to carry 30/60/25 liability coverage. The $30,000 limit pays for injuries to one person if you cause an accident. The $60,000 limit covers total injuries for everyone involved in that accident. The $25,000 portion pays for damage you cause to another person's car or property. In a city like Houston, where medical bills and repair costs are high, limits can run out quickly.

Coverage What It Covers What It Doesn't Cover
$30,000 Bodily Injury Per Person
Medical bills for one person you injure in an accident
Anything above $30K — you pay the difference out of pocket
$60,000 Bodily Injury Per Accident
Total medical bills for all injured parties in one accident
Costs exceeding $60K when multiple people are hurt
$25,000 Property Damage
Repairs to the other driver's vehicle or property
Your own vehicle repairs, your medical bills, or your passengers' injuries

Is Minimum Coverage Enough for Houston Drivers?

Minimum coverage may meet Texas legal requirements, but it often falls short for Houston drivers. With heavy traffic, frequent accidents, and rising repair and medical costs, even a minor crash can quickly exceed basic limits. In a city where vehicle values and healthcare expenses are high, liability claims can add up fast. Storm-related risks like flooding and hail can also increase overall damage costs. Without higher coverage, drivers may face significant out-of-pocket expenses after an accident.
